Security & the threat model

What DeskGuest is built to withstand, what each defense is, and — just as important — what it does not claim to protect against.

What is being protected

Your device and everything on it; the screen and input during a session; your identity key and your ways back in; and the Activity Record that proves what happened.

Who it is protected against

The fake helper

A scammer who phones or messages claiming to be support and asks you to "let them in." Defense: a session can only begin when you open DeskGuest and hand out a one-time code; the helper is named on screen the whole time; and DeskGuest never presses Yes on system prompts. Is this a scam?

A helper who oversteps

A paired person or AI agent doing more than you allowed. Defense: per-helper permission levels set by you; sensitive actions arrive as approval cards before they run; presence that cannot be hidden; STOP that cuts input first; and an Activity Record that can't be quietly edited.

Someone watching the wire

An observer on the network, or a relay carrying your session. Defense: sessions are sealed between the devices. Relays and coordination services can carry a session but cannot read one; the swarm phone-book finds devices without any server knowing the list.

A lost or stolen device

Defense: your identity is a key held on the device, and any surviving device of yours can strike a lost device's key from your circle. Recovery doors (a sealed file, a trusted person, split pieces, a word sheet) restore you — never a central reset DeskGuest controls.

The consent floor

Four properties are the product, not settings — no policy, admin, or helper can switch them off: the connected helper is named on every screen; STOP always works; DeskGuest refuses to type credentials and never clicks through system permission prompts; and the Activity Record cannot be quietly edited.

Never, by design

  • ✓No telemetry or analytics — the build contains no analytics code to switch off.
  • ✓No credential is ever readable back — under any approval, by any interface.
  • ✓No mining your messages, call logs, photos, or clipboard history to build context.
  • ✓No network interception, packet capture, or traffic manipulation tooling.
  • ✓No silent permission grants — grants happen only through the owner's gates.
  • ✓No pre-bundled third-party keys or metered allowances shipped inside the product.
  • ✓No fake capability — a thing a device cannot do reports unavailable, with a reason.

What DeskGuest does not claim

Stated plainly, so nobody has to guess:

It can't judge your trust for you

If you hand a code to the wrong person, DeskGuest shows you who is connected and stops them the instant you say so — but the choice of who to trust is yours. The scam page exists to help with exactly that.

It doesn't protect a device that's already compromised

If malicious software already runs on a device with full rights, no remote-help tool can make that device safe. DeskGuest's record will still show what happened in its own sessions.

Approved actions are the approver's

When you tap Approve on an action card, DeskGuest runs it and records your approval. Read cards before approving them — that's what they're for.

Support is proven where it's proven

Security claims are made at the level they have been tested: Windows 11 and the Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra today. The IT page states the support matrix exactly.

Found a security problem? Please report it privately through GitHub's vulnerability reporting for the DeskGuest project. Please don't post security problems publicly before they've had a chance to be fixed.
