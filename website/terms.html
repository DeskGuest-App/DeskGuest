9. Recording and transcription: the recorder carries the duty

In short: If you record or transcribe a session, getting every participant's consent — under the law where each of them is — is your legal duty, not the tool's.

DeskGuest can record sessions and transcribe session audio. Transcription is recording — a transcript is a record of the conversation, and these rules apply to it in full.

Recording law varies by place, and in some places — including Florida, where DeskGuest is published (Florida Statutes § 934.03) — every participant in a conversation must consent to its recording. Sessions routinely cross state and national lines, and the strictest law that applies to any participant governs.

DeskGuest therefore works on an all-party-consent basis: when recording or transcription is on, every participant is told, sees a persistent indicator that cannot be hidden, and is given the choice to consent or leave. But the tools do not discharge your legal duty. If you record or transcribe, you are responsible for making sure every participant consents, under the law where each participant is located.

Recordings and transcripts are stored on the device of the person who made them. We never receive them. That person is responsible for storing them safely, keeping them only as long as they should, and for any duties the content triggers (for example, an employer's or a school's rules).
