Rooms & live collaboration

Fixing a machine? Start a session. Gathering people? Host a room. Working with the same group again and again? Use a room — it keeps that group's sessions, members and records in one place.

Pre-release. DeskGuest v0.0.1 is a release candidate and is not publicly released yet. This page describes how Rooms and sessions are designed to work. Items marked "in development" are not in the release candidate.

Session or room?

A session is help on one device. A room is a standing meeting space for a group that meets more than once, can involve several devices, and keeps its records together. A session started inside a room works exactly like one started on its own. Today a session has one helper. Sessions with several people and AI agents (watching, pointing, taking turns, or driving side by side) are in development.

"Why don't I just always use a session?" If you only ever help one person with one device, you don't need a room.

Nothing to set up before a session

You don't need to create or schedule anything before a session. Start it and the helper is connected to the device.

Live view and chat

The shared screen and text chat are part of every session from the first second — for the person helping, the person being helped, and anyone else taking part.

Every helper named

Your helper — a person or an AI agent — takes part under their own name and permissions, named in the presence layer for the whole session. An AI can join just to watch, take notes, and point things out — without driving. Several named participants in one session together is in development.

Pointing, annotating, noting

Any participant can point at what they mean — point-and-hold annotations on the shared screen — and the session feed narrates each participant's actions as they happen. Shared session notes gather what the group is working out, in the session where it's being worked out.

Guests arrive by invite

Someone outside the group joins the way Quick Fix works: a one-time code, used while the device owner is present, with nothing installed and nothing left behind. How Quick Fix codes work

Working side by side

Two ways to work together in one session are planned: taking turns, or both driving at once with separate named pointers. Taking turns is how sessions work today; the rest is in development.

Floor mode — taking turns

One person has control at a time. Control changes hands only when someone uses Give control or Take control. Moving the mouse or typing does not take it back. Each handover is visible: the name on screen changes and the session record logs it. Requesting control with one tap is in development.

Side-by-side — in development

Two participants driving at the same time, each with their own named, coloured pointer, working on their own surface, and every input attributed to whoever made it in the record. Side-by-side is a core session mode, chosen in the open — never a quiet escalation. It is in development as part of the multi-participant session work; until it lands, sessions run in floor mode.

Watching — in development

Planned: a second operator will be able to shadow a live session — watching, recorded as a watcher, while presence names the driver. Shadowing is how a senior tech supervises, a trainee learns, or a second pair of eyes checks the work.

One STOP stops everyone. STOP stops everything. The device owner's STOP (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F9) ends the whole session at once. Presence names every participant for the entire session; there is no audience invisible to the person being helped.

Rooms: the standing place

A session is the live event; a room is the place it happens in. Rooms are optional — collaboration never requires one — and they're for the work that recurs: the family computer, the project team, the customer site.

Owned by a group, with named members

A room belongs to a group and has named members — the people (and agents) who belong there. Each member's device is paired manually, one at a time. No link adds anyone automatically. The room's membership is the group's to govern, under the group's admins.

A memory of everything that happened

Session records, notes, and receipts are filed in the room where they happened. Months later, "what did we do about that printer?" has an answer with a record attached — not a chat scrollback.

Sessions, your way

A session can be standalone or started inside a room. Scheduling sessions and moving a running session into a room are in development. And a device pulled into a room session stays under its own owner's rules: that device's presence, approvals, and STOP are never diluted by the room it was pulled into.

A receipt for the person helped

After every session — in a room or out of one — a plain-language summary is left on the helped device: who was here, what they did.
