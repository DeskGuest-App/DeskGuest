The three signs of the real thing

1 · You started it

Help begins only when you start it. On the Full Platform, you open DeskGuest and give out a one-time code; with Quick Fix, you open the invite link your helper sent you. DeskGuest never calls, messages, or pops up asking you to let someone in.

2 · You can see them

The helper is named on your screen the whole time, and you see everything they do. No name on screen means no legitimate session.

3 · STOP always works

If STOP doesn't end it immediately, it isn't DeskGuest behaving as designed — end the session and report it.

Red flags — hang up and close it

  • !Someone contacted you first, claiming your computer has a problem
  • !You're asked to install something that isn't from this site or the project's GitHub
  • !You're told to keep the session secret, or to hurry
  • !You're asked for a password, a bank login, or a recovery key — a real helper never needs them, and DeskGuest refuses to type credentials
  • !You're told the permission popup will be "handled for you" — DeskGuest never presses Yes for anyone
  • !There's no name on your screen, or STOP doesn't end it instantly

Any one of these is enough. Ending a session costs nothing and can always be restarted with someone you trust.

If it already happened

Press STOP. If the session won't end, close DeskGuest; if it still won't end, shut the computer down. Then, from a different device if you can, remove that helper in DeskGuest and send a report — the Activity Record on your device shows what happened.

The one-sentence test

Real help starts with you, stays visible to you, and stops the moment you say so. Anything else is not DeskGuest — whatever the person on the phone claims.
