Why DeskGuest Exists

Help should happen in the open.

DeskGuest exists because getting help with a device came to mean handing your screen to a black box — a company’s account, a company’s cloud, a session you can’t really watch. We’re building the opposite: help that happens in front of you, from someone — or something — named on your screen, with your hand on STOP.

The problem we kept seeing

Nothing here is a complaint about any one product — the established names are good at what they do. It’s about the shape the whole field settled into, and what that shape asks of the person being helped.

Help arrives as a black box

Before anyone can help, there’s an account to create and a company’s cloud in the middle of the session. While they help, what you get is a small window and their word for it. The person whose machine it is — the one with the most at stake — sees the least.

Three tools for one job

One app to get help right now. Another to share a screen in a meeting. A standing account with each, a subscription with each, and a different set of rules for who’s in charge in each. The job never changed — someone you trust, helping on your device — but the toolbox kept splitting.

And now the helper is an AI

AI agents can already operate a computer — and they’re becoming the helpers. The established tools were built for human helpers and company fleets; none of them was built to keep you in charge of an agent: named on screen, working inside a leash you set, stoppable in a second, and written down afterwards.

So DeskGuest starts from the other end. The device’s owner is the boss of every connection: the helper is named, the session is watchable, the record stays on the device — and no account or company cloud stands between you and the person helping you.

One product, three kinds of help

What people split across several tools today, DeskGuest sets out to do in one place — under one set of rules.

Help in the moment

Something’s broken and someone you trust can fix it — a family member, a friend, your IT person. One invite, they’re in, you watch everything, and it ends when you say so. The job TeamViewer and AnyDesk do today. Quick Fix, in full

Rooms for your people

A standing place where your people gather — share a screen, talk, help each other — without booking a meeting or renting one by the seat. The job Zoom and Teams do today. Rooms, in full

Your AI, as a guest

Pair the AI agent you already use. It connects as itself, works under the access you grant, asks before anything sensitive, and leaves a record under its own name. The job nobody’s tool was built for. AI agents, in full

One set of rules for all three: the helper is named on screen, you approve what’s sensitive, STOP always works, and everything is written down on your device. And the terms never change underneath you: free, open source, no account, no DeskGuest cloud. That isn’t a tier of the product — it is the product. The full feature tour

What DeskGuest costs — and what it doesn’t. DeskGuest is free and charges nothing: no account, no subscription, no per-session fee. If you pair an AI agent, its plan is between you and its maker — DeskGuest holds no provider keys and keeps no telemetry.

Built in the open, on purpose.

DeskGuest is an open-source project. The code, the open issues, and the roadmap live in public — if you want to know what we intend, you don’t have to take this page’s word for it. DeskGuest is in development now; downloads open with the first release.
