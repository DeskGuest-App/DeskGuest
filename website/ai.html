From a request to a record

One worked example — you on your phone, your agent acting on your computer. This sequence is the whole arrangement in miniature: ask, approve, and it's written down.

Step 1

You ask

From your phone, in the agent's chat thread inside DeskGuest: “The printer on the office PC keeps failing — find out why.” A message alone grants nothing and starts nothing.

Step 2

You approve

The agent's request comes back as an action card naming the target computer and exactly what it wants to do. Your one tap approves that — and nothing beyond it. Anything sensitive stops at a card again.

Step 3

It’s recorded

The agent works on the PC under full presence — its name in the Box, its ghost pointer on screen, STOP within reach. Afterwards, that PC's Activity Record shows every step under the agent's own name, and the session replays.

Supported AIs

DeskGuest is built to work with any AI agent that uses MCP, the open connection standard — the same door, the same rules, for all of them. An agent marked Fully supported has been paired with DeskGuest and put through its paces end to end: pairing and acknowledging the terms (recorded, not yet binding — see Terms of Service v1.0), staying inside the access it was given, watching, being refused and then allowed at an approval prompt, doing real work, stopping the instant STOP is pressed, ending its own session, and leaving a clean record under its own name. An agent marked Not yet tried hasn't been tried with DeskGuest yet — its maker documents the connection, and we take their word for it until we've seen it ourselves. New agents move up as we get to try them.

Bring your own agentPairing, identity, and revocation

DeskGuest doesn't come with an AI inside it, and it never phones one on your behalf. You bring the agent you already use and pair it with your device.

Any standards-based agent

DeskGuest connects through MCP, the open standard AI agents use to work with other software. Any agent that uses it can pair. No agent gets special treatment because of who made it.

It connects as itself

At pairing, the agent gives its name, and that name is kept on the pairing and shown everywhere the connection appears. With a face you choose for it, name and face ride the pairing as a presentation card — the device being helped always knows exactly who is here.

Pairing is always your act

Pairing starts on your device, with a one-time code the app shows you. An agent can never pair itself, pair another agent, or widen its own access — not at pairing, not ever. Each pairing gets its own token, kept safely on your device: if one ever leaked, it would affect that pairing alone, and you can cancel it on its own.

No codes carried in your head

Where a step happens inside your AI's own app, DeskGuest generates a one-tap copy prompt on your device, from the live pairing state, carrying everything the agent needs. You never compose instructions for your AI, and never carry codes between apps in your head.

It knows exactly what it may use

When an agent connects, DeskGuest tells it exactly what this device and this pairing allow — and why the rest is off-limits. An agent never has to guess how far it may go, and anything a device can't do says so plainly, with a reason.

Revocation is one tap

Remove a pairing and it ends on the agent's next action — not at the next sync, not eventually. Pairings can also carry an expiry, and stop working the moment it passes. Across a whole fleet, "revoke this agent everywhere" shows you the full list of what changes before you confirm.

AI is optional, completely

Everything else in DeskGuest — sessions, rooms, Quick Fix, fleets — works with no AI involved at all. An agent is a guest you invite, on a leash you set. A device with no paired agent loses nothing.

Permissions on each deviceKinds of access, approvals, time-boxed grants

Right after pairing, DeskGuest asks one plain question: "What may this agent do?" Your answer is stored per pairing — per device, per agent — can be re-tuned at any time, and tunes capability only. The protections underneath it — being seen, being stoppable — never move.

Look only

The starting point for every new pairing. The agent can observe — see the screen, read device state, follow along — and can do nothing else until you say so.

Full control

One tap grants the working set: driving, files, and system tasks. The sensitive things — payments, sending, deleting, installing — still stop and ask you first, in every mode, including this one.

Custom

Four kinds of access, granted independently: observe (see), drive (tap, type, gesture), shell (run commands and system work), and admin (the deepest system tasks). Mix them per agent: your phone agent might drive, your work agent might only observe.

How approvals work

Per pairing, you choose the rhythm: approve every action, approve a plan and let the steps run, or trust the agent inside its grant. Whichever you choose, the sensitive things always stop and ask — an approval mode can relax the small things, never the things that matter.

Time-boxed grants

Access can be bounded by the clock: this agent, these hours, until Friday. Outside the window the grant is inactive. Expiry is automatic, written into the record, and never silently extended.

Sessions end themselves

You can time-box a session, and a session with no agent action for a set interval closes itself and says so. When a task is finished, the agent ends the session as its final action — the presence clearing from your screen is your "done" signal.

Working style, inside the grant

You can tune how an agent works without touching what it may do: how much it narrates, whether it asks first or acts first inside its grant, how detailed its session notes are. Style is yours to set; the grant itself only ever changes by your hand.

How the agent reaches you is also part of the pairing: Auto tries every way in and switches quietly when one fails; Auto, restricted uses only the ways in you allow — never the public ones; Manual sticks to one. The way in an agent is actually using is always shown, and if the allowed ways in all fail, DeskGuest says so honestly instead of quietly widening its reach. Every way in is encrypted, and the agent checks it is really your computer against the fingerprint recorded when you paired them. The unencrypted way in is refused by default — a setting an IT department can lock in place — and when two devices talk to each other, that conversation is sealed end to end however it travels. How your agent seesStructure, pictures, and a live feed

An agent that can only stare at pixels guesses. DeskGuest gives it the screen the way the device itself understands it — and is honest about the edges of that understanding.

The screen, as structure

The agent's primary sense is a structured snapshot: the screen as a tree of labelled elements — buttons, fields, lists — each with a stable reference. It acts on elements by reference, not by hoping a coordinate is right. If a reference has gone stale, the action fails and asks for a fresh snapshot. It never guesses a tap.

Pictures, inside a session only

The agent can take a screenshot, and an annotated one with the interactive elements labelled and numbered — but only inside a live session, while the presence layer is up. There is no way to capture your screen quietly, and no background-monitoring mode exists.

Screens, read into data

"Read this screen into data" returns the visible content of an app as structured fields — inputs with their values, actions, text — instead of a paragraph of prose. Password values are always redacted before anything leaves the device.

Device state, in one report

Model, system version, battery, network, storage, screen size and which way up it is, and the app that's currently open — the agent can ask for the facts before it acts.

A live feed, not snapshots on demand

During a session the agent can follow along on a live feed: pictures that adapt to what's moving, combined with changes to the screen's layout and the session's events. It can point and annotate — "look here" — without driving. The honest limit: the feed is continuous but machine-paced — sampled motion with exact structure, never claimed to be human-speed vision.

Some apps are invisible

Apps you mark — banking, password managers, anything you choose — cannot be snapshotted, screenshotted, driven, or even listed for an agent. And while you have taken over the screen yourself, for a login or anything private, capture suspends entirely: the record shows a takeover happened, never what it contained.

How your agent actsDriving, system work, and the hard refusals

Inside its grant, a paired agent can do real work — the same work a careful person at the keyboard could do, done in the open, one visible flow at a time.

Driving

  • On Windows: mouse, keyboard, and commands
  • On the Android companion: tap, long-press, and swipe — by element or by coordinate
  • Multi-touch gestures, with two virtual fingers rendered on screen
  • On the Android companion: hardware keys: back, home, recents, volume
  • Text typed into the focused field; apps launched; deep links opened
  • Every contact lands with a ripple from the agent's ghost pointer — you see each action as it happens

Work in batches, waiting honestly

A sequence of actions can run as one batch — one visible flow, one record entry per step. Waits are first-class: the agent can wait for an element, a text, an app state, or a duration, and a timeout reports as a timeout. Nothing pretends to have happened that didn't.

System work, behind the glass

With the run-commands access, the agent works through a channel the device opened for itself — nothing comes in from outside, and administrator-level tasks are only available if you grant the admin access — and even then, each one asks first.

  • Commands are grouped by how powerful they are, and the powerful ones ask first; what comes back is capped; everything is written down
  • When it changes a setting, DeskGuest reads the setting back to be sure the change really happened — if it didn't, that's reported as a failure, not a success
  • Installing an app always asks first; removing or disabling one asks in the same way
  • Files listed, sent, received, moved, and deleted within the device's storage rules
  • A reboot, when you approve it, ends the session; the device restores its own channel after your next unlock

What it can never do

Some refusals have no override. An agent can never type into a password, one-time-code, or PIN field — no approval can enable it. It can never grant permissions to other apps as a side effect. And on a locked machine, screen control reports itself unavailable and command-side access states its own limits: there is no unlock trick, and DeskGuest doesn't pretend otherwise.

Talk to your agent — and give it ordersChat, action cards, command from your phone

Every pairing has a chat thread inside DeskGuest, living with the agent's card. Messaging grants no capability and raises no presence — it's a conversation. To have the agent do something, ask in the thread; it replies with an action card for you to approve.

Orders become action cards

Tell your agent what you want in plain words. When it's time to act, the agent's request arrives as a structured action card in the conversation — start a session, send this file, change this setting — and your one tap is the approval that creates the record. Free text never executes anything. During a live session, chat orders steer the agent inside its grant; anything beyond the grant raises the same approval card in the chat.

Command from your phone

The conversation isn't tied to the device in your hand. Open DeskGuest on your phone and direct a paired agent that acts on your computer — or any device it's paired to. The rules travel with the order: the agent acts under that computer's own pairing and leash, the full presence shows on that computer, the action card names it, approvals come to your phone, and you hold a remote STOP there alongside the computer's own STOP.

Honest about reachability

An agent answers instantly only when its own side is awake and connected. DeskGuest shows each agent's true state — reachable now, last seen, or will deliver when it wakes — and queues messages rather than pretending they landed. The DeskGuest thread is an extra way to reach the agent; its own app still works.

Small things, done politely

An agent can place text on your clipboard for one-tap pasting (reading your clipboard always asks first). It can post you a notification — a question, a completion, a warning — instead of acting silently. With an opt-in grant it can read incoming notifications and flag anything urgent by a small set of built-in rules, always saying which rule flagged it. It can read a web page as text, consult per-app guidance notes before driving an unfamiliar app, and — as an opt-in module, indicated when used — trigger a full-resolution photo into a folder you chose, for the nameplate or serial tag you can't quite reach.

While it works, you're still in the roomPresence, voice, and Background Work Only

Everything DeskGuest promises about presence for a human helper applies to an agent in full — the same Box naming it, the same ghost pointer, the same glow, the same STOP. An agent session is never a quieter kind of session.

Background Work Only

Need your screen back mid-task? One toggle — yours, never the agent's — holds all screen work: held items queue in order, visibly counted, and are never auto-replayed later. Work that doesn't need the screen continues. The banner stays up the whole time.

A running commentary

A persistent activity line states in plain words what the agent is doing as it acts, and session sounds announce the start and the end. A session never begins or ends silently.

While you're driving, it doesn't (Android companion)

In a vehicle, screen driving is refused outright. Voice-grade interactions — notifying you, reading aloud — remain available. The device reports where it is; an agent gets no tools to make it lie about that, or about anything else.

A voice for your agent

Paired agents can speak and listen in a session. By default the voice is built in and local: it runs on the device, works offline, and nothing about it phones home — DeskGuest holds no AI provider keys, ever. You can swap in a different voice pack per agent, and when an agent brings a voice from its own platform, its card says so — you always know whose voice is speaking. The honest expectation: the built-in voice is good and natural, a notch below the best cloud voices; nothing leaves your device to close that gap.

Saved routines

A sequence an agent performs often can be saved as a named routine and replayed on demand — on Windows (V1 platform). A routine is exactly what its author supplied, step for step; DeskGuest doesn't record your screen sessions into routines, and doesn't claim to.

Everything is written down, under its nameThe Activity Record and replay

Trust doesn't come from the agent's promises. It comes from a record the agent can't touch, kept on your device, in plain language.

The Activity Record

Every action an agent takes — what it was, where, when, and what you approved along the way — is appended to an on-device record under the agent's own name. Agents cannot alter or delete it. Anything known to be sensitive is blanked out on the device before any output leaves it, and the record exports only from inside the app, by you.

Replay the session

Any session can be replayed in the app: the timeline, the pointer's path, the actions, the gate events — what happened, in the order it happened. If an agent's work is ever in question, you don't have to take anyone's word for it. Trust & security, in full

The responsibility line, stated plainly: you are responsible for what you allow an agent to do — the grant is your act. What DeskGuest guarantees is the frame around that act: total visibility while it works, approvals before anything sensitive, a STOP that always works, and a complete record afterwards.
