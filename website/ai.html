Your AI, on your devices, under your rules

DeskGuest lets you pair your own AI agent with your phone and your computers, so it can genuinely help — see the screen, do the work, fix the problem — inside a frame of presence, permissions, and records that you control. This page is the whole arrangement, in detail.

Bring your own agent

DeskGuest doesn't come with an AI inside it, and it never phones one on your behalf. You bring the agent you already use — DeskGuest is the door it walks through, and the door is yours.

Any standards-based agent

DeskGuest's control surface is built on MCP, the open standard AI agents use to work with tools. Any agent that speaks it can pair. No agent is privileged because of who made it — the same door, the same rules, for all of them.

It connects as itself

At pairing, the agent gives its name, and that name is kept on the pairing and shown everywhere the connection appears. With a face you choose for it, the name and face ride the pairing as a presentation card — so the device being helped always knows exactly who is here.

Pairing is always your act

Pairing starts on your device, with a one-time code the app shows you. An agent can never pair itself, pair another agent, or widen its own access — not at pairing, not ever. Each pairing gets its own token, held in your device's keystore: one leak compromises one pairing, and you can revoke it in isolation.

No codes carried in your head

Where a step happens inside your AI's own app, DeskGuest generates a one-tap copy prompt on your device, from the live pairing state, carrying everything the agent needs. You never compose instructions for your AI, and never carry codes between apps in your head.

It knows exactly what it may use

When an agent connects, DeskGuest tells it precisely which capabilities this device and this pairing support — and why the rest are unavailable. An agent never has to guess at its own reach, and a capability a device can't perform reports unavailable, with a reason.

Revocation is one tap

Remove a pairing and it ends on the agent's next action — not at the next sync, not eventually. Pairings can also carry an expiry and fail closed when it passes. Across a whole fleet, "revoke this agent everywhere" shows you the full list of what changes before you confirm.

AI is optional, completely

Everything else in DeskGuest — sessions, rooms, Quick Fix, fleets — works with no AI involved at all. An agent is a guest you invite, on a leash you set. A device with no paired agent loses nothing.

The leash: you decide what it may do

Right after pairing, DeskGuest asks one plain question: "What may this agent do?" Your answer is stored per pairing, can be re-tuned at any time, and tunes capability only — the consent floor underneath it never moves.

Look only

The starting point for every new pairing. The agent can observe — see the screen, read device state, follow along — and can do nothing else until you say so.

Full control

One tap grants the working set: driving, files, and system tasks. The sensitive classes — payments, sends, deletions, installs — still stop and ask you first, in every mode, including this one.

Custom

Four scopes, granted independently: observe (see), drive (tap, type, gesture), shell (commands and system work), and admin (the deepest system tasks). Mix them per agent: your phone agent might drive, your work agent might only observe.

How approvals work

Per pairing, you choose the rhythm: approve every action, approve a plan and let the steps run, or trust the agent inside its grant. Whichever you choose, the gated classes stay gated — an approval mode can relax the small things, never the things that matter.

Time-boxed grants

Access can be bounded by the clock: this agent, these hours, until Friday. Outside the window the grant doesn't merely sleep — it doesn't exist. Expiry is automatic, written into the record, and never silently extended.

Sessions end themselves

You can time-box a session, and a session with no agent action for a set interval closes itself and says so. When a task is finished, the agent ends the session as its final action — the presence clearing from your screen is your "done" signal.

Working style, inside the grant

You can tune how an agent works without touching what it may do: how much it narrates, whether it asks first or acts first inside its grant, how detailed its session notes are. Style is yours to set; the grant itself only ever changes by your hand.

How the agent reaches you is also part of the pairing: Auto works every connection road and fails over silently; Auto, restricted works only the subset of roads you allow (your own network and mesh, say — never the public ones); Manual pins one road. The road an agent is actually on is always shown, and a restricted set with no working road reports that honestly instead of quietly widening. The connection roads, in the full tour

How your agent sees

An agent that can only stare at pixels guesses. DeskGuest gives it the screen the way the device itself understands it — and is honest about the edges of that understanding.

The screen, as structure

The agent's primary sense is a structured snapshot: the screen as a tree of labelled elements — buttons, fields, lists — each with a stable reference. It acts on elements by reference, not by hoping a coordinate is right. If a reference has gone stale, the action fails and asks for a fresh snapshot. It never guesses a tap.

Pictures, inside a session only

The agent can take a screenshot, and an annotated one with the interactive elements labelled and numbered — but only inside a live session, while the presence layer is up. There is no way to capture your screen quietly, and no background-monitoring mode exists.

Screens, read into data

"Read this screen into data" returns the visible content of an app as structured fields — inputs with their values, actions, text — instead of a paragraph of prose. Password values are always redacted before anything leaves the device.

Device state, in one report

Model, OS build, battery, network, storage, display size and orientation, the foreground app and what's focused — the agent can ask for the facts before it acts, and every snapshot header carries the current app and focus.

A live feed, not snapshots on demand

During a session the agent can follow along on a live perception feed: frames that adapt to what's moving, fused with changes to the screen's structure and the session's events. It can point and annotate — "look here" — without driving. The honest limit: the feed is continuous but machine-paced — sampled motion with exact structure, never claimed to be human-speed vision.

Some apps are invisible

Apps you mark — banking, password managers, anything you choose — cannot be snapshotted, screenshotted, driven, or even listed for an agent. And while you have taken over the screen yourself, for a login or anything private, capture suspends entirely: the record shows a takeover happened, never what it contained.

How your agent acts

Inside its grant, a paired agent can do real work — the same work a careful person at the keyboard could do, done in the open, one visible flow at a time.

Driving

Tap, long-press, and swipe by element or coordinate; multi-touch gestures — pinch, two-finger scroll — with two virtual fingers rendered on screen; hardware keys (back, home, recents, volume); text typed into the focused field; apps launched, deep links opened; the display rotated or refolded where the hardware allows. Every contact lands with a ripple from the agent's ghost pointer, so you see each action as it happens.

Work in batches, waiting honestly

A sequence of actions can run as one batch — one visible flow, one record entry per step. Waits are first-class: the agent can wait for an element, a text, an app state, or a duration, and a timeout reports as a timeout. Nothing pretends to have happened that didn't.

System work, behind the glass

With the shell scope, the agent works the device's own shell — a channel the device paired to itself, with no external lane and no root. Commands are gate-classed, output is capped, and everything is logged. Settings it changes are verified by reading them back: an unverified write reports failure, not success. Installing an app is a gated class; removing or disabling one goes through the same gates. Files can be listed, sent, received, moved, and deleted within the device's storage rules; processes can be listed, focused, and ended under gates; filtered device logs and the app's own crash reports are readable for diagnosis; and a reboot, when you approve it, ends the session — the device restores its own channel after your next unlock.

What it can never do

Some refusals have no override. An agent can never type into a password, one-time-code, or PIN field — no approval can enable it. It can never grant permissions to other apps as a side effect. And on a locked machine, screen control reports itself unavailable and command-side access states its own limits: there is no unlock trick, and DeskGuest doesn't pretend otherwise.

Talk to your agent — and give it orders

Every pairing has a chat thread inside DeskGuest, living with the agent's card. Messaging grants no capability and raises no presence — it's a conversation. What makes it powerful is what happens when you ask for action.

Orders become action cards

Tell your agent what you want in plain words. When it's time to act, the agent's request arrives as a structured action card in the conversation — start a session, send this file, change this setting — and your one tap is the approval that creates the record. Free text never executes anything. During a live session, chat orders steer the agent inside its grant; anything beyond the grant raises the same approval card in the chat.

Command from your phone

The conversation isn't tied to the device in your hand. Open DeskGuest on your phone and direct a paired agent that acts on your computer — or any device it's paired to. The rules travel with the order: the agent acts under the target device's own pairing and leash, presence fires in full on the target, the action card names the target device, approvals come to your phone, and you hold a remote STOP there alongside the target's own STOP.

Honest about reachability

An agent answers instantly only when its own side is awake and connected. DeskGuest shows each agent's true state — reachable now, last seen, or will deliver when it wakes — and queues messages rather than pretending they landed. The same agent is equally reachable in its own app; the DeskGuest thread is an extra front door, never a trap.

Small things, done politely

An agent can place text on your clipboard for one-tap pasting (reading your clipboard is gate-classed). It can post you a notification — a question, a completion, a warning — instead of acting silently. With an opt-in grant it can read incoming notifications and flag urgency by a small set of built-in rules, every verdict naming the rule that produced it. It can read a web page as text, consult per-app guidance notes before driving an unfamiliar app, and — as an opt-in module, indicated when used — trigger a full-resolution photo into a folder you chose, for the nameplate or serial tag you can't quite reach.

While it works, you're still in the room

Everything the presence law promises for a human helper applies to an agent in full — the same Box naming it, the same ghost pointer, the same glow, the same STOP. An agent session is never a quieter kind of session.

Background Work Only

Need your screen back mid-task? One toggle — yours, never the agent's — holds all screen work: held items queue in order, visibly counted, and are never auto-replayed later. Work that doesn't need the screen continues. The banner stays up the whole time.

A running commentary

A persistent activity line states in plain words what the agent is doing as it acts, and session sounds announce the start and the end. A session never begins or ends silently.

While you're driving, it doesn't

In a vehicle, screen driving is refused outright. Voice-grade interactions — notifying you, reading aloud — remain available. The device reports where it is; an agent gets no tools to make it lie about that, or about anything else.

A voice for your agent

Paired agents can speak and listen in a session. By default the voice is built in and local: it runs on the device, works offline, and nothing about it phones home — DeskGuest holds no AI provider keys, ever. You can swap in a different voice pack per agent, and when an agent brings a voice from its own platform, its card says so — you always know whose voice is speaking. The honest expectation: the built-in voice is good and natural, a notch below the best cloud voices; nothing leaves your device to close that gap.

Saved routines

A sequence an agent performs often can be saved as a named routine and replayed on demand — on Windows today. A routine is exactly what its author supplied, step for step; DeskGuest doesn't record your screen sessions into routines, and doesn't claim to.

Everything is written down, under its name

Trust doesn't come from the agent's promises. It comes from a record the agent can't touch, kept on your device, in plain language.

The Activity Record

Every action an agent takes — what it was, where, when, which gate it passed and what you approved — is appended to an on-device record under the agent's own name. Agents cannot alter or delete it. Known-sensitive values are redacted on the device before any output leaves it, and the record exports only from inside the app, by you.

Replay the session

Any session can be replayed in the app: the timeline, the pointer's path, the actions, the gate events — what happened, in the order it happened. If an agent's work is ever in question, you don't have to take anyone's word for it. Trust & security, in full

The responsibility line, stated plainly: you are responsible for what you allow an agent to do — the grant is your act. What DeskGuest guarantees is the frame around that act: total visibility while it works, approvals before anything sensitive, a STOP that always works, and a complete record afterwards.
